What this qualification is

It's the DVSA-required management qualification for running an MOT testing station. Every Authorised Examiner must have at least one trained manager (an AEDM or AEP) — this is the qualification that makes a person professionally competent to fill that role. Once passed, it's a lifetime qualification.

The four units it covers

UnitWhat it's about
1. Legislation & complianceRunning a VTS within the rules — equipment, calibration, records, forms, appeals
2. Customer serviceHandling complaints and problems professionally
3. Staff development & supervisionRecruiting, appraising, training and supervising testers
4. Quality systems & auditSite assessments, quality control checks, risk and disciplinary points
The skill being tested isn't memory — it's judgement + fast lookup. With 35 questions in 75 minutes you have about 2 minutes each, so the open book is a backstop for technical detail, not something to lean on for every question.

What you can look up vs what you must know

This is the single most important thing to understand. The open book only helps with roughly half the exam — the technical units. The "softer" units aren't in the guide and have to be learned.

✅ Look it up

In the guide you're allowed

  • Calibration intervals & record retention
  • Appeals timeframes & VT forms
  • Disciplinary points (Appendix 8)
  • Quality control check frequency
  • Pass / Major / Dangerous test outcomes
  • Decline vs Refuse rules

🧠 Must memorise

Not in the guide

  • Health & Safety law — HSWA 1974, "reasonably practicable", noise limits
  • Hazard vs risk definitions
  • Staff management — appraisals, training plans
  • Customer-care principles — communication
  • Current training cycle (3 hrs/year — not the old 5-year rule)

Find it in the guide (open-book day)

For the look-up topics, here's exactly where the answer lives and the Ctrl+F term to jump to it. Practise finding 3–4 of these live before the exam — speed is the whole game.

Appeals, complaints & forms
"…no later than 14 working days after the date of the test [for a fail]… 28 calendar days for a wrongful pass… 3 months if corrosion-related." DVSA re-exam offered within 5 working days.
Ctrl+F: working days, VT17, corrosion
Appeal an MOT test result (VT17)
Equipment, calibration & records
Decelerometer: not exceeding 1 year. Roller/plate brake & headlamp aim testers: within the 6 months after last calibration. Diesel smoke meter: weekly user check + 12-monthly UKAS calibration. Calibration records: kept 2 years minimum.
Ctrl+F: calibrat, decelerometer, two years
MOT Testing Guide — Appendix 2: Facilities & security
Roles & quality control
AEP = legal responsibility; AEDM = day-to-day management; AE = the authorised entity. Quality control: check at least one test per tester every 2 months. Replace a departed manager within 35 working days or stop testing.
Ctrl+F: AEDM, every 2 months, 35 working days
Manage your MOT centre
Discipline & penalty points
Fraud/dishonesty (e.g. dishonestly issuing a VT20, a "ghost" MOT) = 500 points = cessation, can be immediate. Tester cessation at 500 or 100+ accumulated; AE at 500 or 200+. Stop testing immediately if mandatory equipment malfunctions.
Ctrl+F: 500 points, cessation, malfunction
MOT Testing Guide — Appendix 8: Disciplinary procedures
Pass / Major / Dangerous & Decline vs Refuse (scenarios)
Map the scenario to a numbered manual section (e.g. 4. Lamps, 1. Brakes), then Ctrl+F a keyword. Decline = you choose not to start (vehicle unsuitable for facilities). Refuse = a condition/safety reason at pre-check stops the test.
Ctrl+F: section keyword, then decline / refuse
